# Shipping AI products that do not embarrass you

AI demos are easy. AI products are harder because real customers bring messy inputs, edge cases, privacy expectations, budget limits, and support questions.

The market is moving fast. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey reports that 88% of respondents say their organizations use AI regularly in at least one business function, while only about one-third say their companies have begun scaling AI programs across the enterprise. That gap is where most failed AI features live.

## The difference between a demo and a product

A demo proves the model can do something impressive once. A product proves the system can do something useful repeatedly.

That means you need:

- A narrow job-to-be-done, not a generic chatbot.
- A human fallback for risky or unclear cases.
- Evals that catch bad outputs before customers do.
- Cost and latency budgets per workflow.
- Logging that shows what the model saw, decided, and returned.

## Start with workflow fit

Before choosing a model, map the workflow. Ask where the user starts, what data the AI needs, what action happens next, and who checks the result.

Good early AI use cases usually share three traits:

1. The task is repetitive but not fully deterministic.
2. The business already has useful data or examples.
3. A wrong answer can be reviewed, corrected, or blocked.

If the task is vague and the data is scattered, build the workflow first. The model will not rescue a broken process.

## Evals before launch

Every AI feature should ship with a small test set:

- Golden prompts from real business scenarios.
- Adversarial prompts that try to force leakage or unsafe claims.
- Expected response rules in plain language.
- Regression checks after every prompt or model change.

The eval set does not need to be huge on day one. It needs to exist, grow, and be treated like product code.

## Guardrails that matter

Guardrails are not one thing. They are layers:

- Input filters for sensitive or irrelevant requests.
- Retrieval boundaries so the model answers from approved material.
- Output validation for numbers, links, formats, and claims.
- Escalation paths when confidence is low.
- A kill switch for any flow that behaves badly in production.

## What Finlec usually builds first

For small and mid-size businesses, we usually start with one of these:

- Website lead qualification with CRM handoff.
- WhatsApp support triage with template-safe follow-ups.
- Internal knowledge search across policies, services, and pricing.
- Sales proposal draft generation from structured inputs.
- Analytics summaries that explain what changed and why.
