# A WCAG 2.2 checklist for modern redesigns

Accessibility is not a separate layer added after design. It is part of whether the website works.

W3C's WCAG 2.2 guidance is built around four ideas: people should be able to see, use, understand, and rely on the site. For business sites, that turns into many practical design and development checks.

## Design checks

Before handoff, review:

- Text contrast on cards, buttons, overlays, and images.
- Button labels that explain the action.
- Touch targets that are large enough on mobile.
- Focus states that are visible.
- Forms with clear labels and error messages.
- Motion that does not block reading or interaction.

## Development checks

During development, verify:

1. Headings follow a logical order.
2. Images have meaningful alt text when needed.
3. Decorative images are hidden from assistive tech.
4. Navigation works by keyboard.
5. Form errors are connected to inputs.
6. Responsive layouts do not overlap or clip text.

## Why businesses should care

Accessible websites usually convert better because they are clearer, faster to scan, and easier to use under real-world conditions.

The best version of accessibility is invisible to most users because everything simply works.
